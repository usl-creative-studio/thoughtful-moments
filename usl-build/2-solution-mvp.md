## 4. ORIENTATION DECISION

**Route:** validation-first
**Reason:** Assumption #1 is Low confidence and could redirect price, tier and model. No comparable found sells a DIY-delivered night above $300; every premium comparable is done-for-you and tops out near $1,500. A deadline cannot turn that into a fact. The decisive artifact is a priced landing page with a deposit, which is also what the founder intends to build next, so the pipeline stages 3 to 6 produce the experiment rather than delaying it.
**What would change the route:** 3 of 5 monthly slots reserved with deposits inside 30 days moves the route to value-first after passed experiment. Buyer conversations (deferred) that surface observed spend at this level would raise confidence but not replace the deposit test.
**Desirability orientation consequence:** Pull. The behaviour under test is voluntary payment to reach an identity and an outcome (deposit paid), not relief from a stated pain. The value moment is her reaction on the night. The continuation signal is a second booking or a referral to another man in his circle.
**Active-cap routing consequence:** none.

## 5A. VALIDATION EXPERIMENT

**Experiment status:** planned
**Falsifiable hypothesis:** We believe men in the target profile will reserve one of five monthly slots with a $500 refundable deposit against a $2,500 founding-rate night (standard rate shown as $3,500), where the setup, food, flowers and cleanup are done for him and the massage is done by him, because it costs what he already spends on a night out and delivers the effort she actually wants. We will reconsider the price and the DIY model if fewer than 1 in 5 slots is reserved after 30 days and 100 or more qualified visitors.
**Decision controlled:** whether to buy flagship inventory (massage table, linens, china) and proceed to a value-first release at $2,500 rising to $3,500, or to retest at a lower price or a done-for-you-plus format.
**Participants and payer:** men matching the persona in one launch city (city to be named before the page goes live). Recruitment: founder and partner's warm network first, then a capped paid campaign (about $1,500 of the $10k). Target 100+ qualified visitors. Known bias: warm-network buyers may buy out of goodwill; their deposits count only if they are not personal friends of the founders, and every deposit is tagged warm or cold.
**Test artifact and honest limitation:** one landing page for the flagship experience (the at-home massage night) with the enriched stack, price, slot count and a deposit checkout; the afternoon tea and home-cooked meal shown as also available on request, without inventory. What does not exist: any inventory, any booking software beyond a form and a payment link, any second city.
**Task or offer:** reserve one of five nights this month with a $500 refundable deposit, then take a 20-minute consultation call to plan the night.
**Primary observed behaviour:** deposit paid. Consultation requests and email sign-ups are recorded as secondary signals, never as success.
**Success threshold:** 3 or more of 5 slots reserved with deposits within 30 days of launch, at least 2 of them from cold (non-network) visitors.
**Failure threshold:** 0 deposits after 30 days with 100 or more qualified visitors.
**Inconclusive condition:** 1 or 2 deposits, or fewer than 100 qualified visitors in 30 days. Fewer than 100 visitors is a channel result, not a price result: retest the channel (a different audience or a referral push) before touching the price. 1 or 2 deposits with 100+ visitors: run the deferred buyer conversations using the page as the prop, then retest at a materially different price or stack.
**Duration or sample rationale:** 30 days matches one monthly slot cycle. Five slots is the founders' own exclusivity cap and their delivery capacity. 100 qualified visitors is the minimum at which a 3% deposit rate is distinguishable from zero.
**Evidence capture:** page analytics (visitors, source, scroll to price, click to deposit); deposit records with warm/cold tag; consultation notes; per-night time and cost log; post-night follow-up message (what she said, would he do it again, who he would tell).
**Safety, privacy, legal, and refund controls:** deposit fully refundable until the consultation confirms the date; page states 5 nights a month and the launch city; consultation collects a signed massage waiver and a contraindication card (pregnancy, injuries, skin sensitivities, allergies to oils or flowers); food supplied by a licensed bakery or caterer; partner's personal details collected only at consultation and only for the night; no claim of licensed therapy.

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| Pass | Route becomes value-first after passed experiment. Buy flagship inventory only. Deliver the five nights, capture time and cost, then scope the second experience and revisit the $3,500 standard rate. Mark Stage 1 Viability for review with the observed evidence. |
| Fail | Do not lower the price first. Retest once as done-for-you-plus (a professional gives the massage; he does the card, the food plating and the flowers) at $1,500 to $2,000. If that also fails, the solution direction is wrong for this buyer; return to Stage 1. |
| Inconclusive | Channel-inconclusive: change the channel, not the offer. Deposit-inconclusive: run buyer conversations with the page as the prop, then one materially different retest. Do not rerun the same page unchanged. |

**Test flow:**
1. Reach: warm-network messages from both founders plus a capped paid campaign targeting the persona in the launch city; every link tagged by source.
2. Page: the flagship night, the enriched stack, what he does and what is done for him, price and founding rate, 5 slots, the deposit call to action.
3. Deposit: $500 via a payment link; confirmation email sets the consultation call and states the refund terms.
4. Consultation (20 minutes): her tastes, allergies, the date, the address, the card he will write, the waiver; deposit becomes non-refundable once the date is confirmed and sourcing begins.
5. Sourcing per booking: bakery or caterer order, flowers, consumables; no capital purchase before pass.
6. Setup: the team transforms the room and leaves before she arrives; he delivers the massage with the cue cards; the team collects and cleans the next morning.
7. Follow-up within 48 hours: what she said, would he book again, who he would tell. Referral ask.
8. Decision gate at day 30 against the thresholds above.

**Contamination risks:** friends of the founders buying out of kindness (tag and exclude from the cold count); a founder present on the night coaching him (the team leaves before she arrives; his part is his alone); a stack so enriched that the massage becomes incidental (the page must say plainly that he gives the massage); ad spend too small to reach 100 qualified visitors, producing a channel result misread as a price result; success threshold changed after seeing results (fixed here).

**Core value moment:** step 6, her reaction to a night he visibly did himself. Evidence: his follow-up in his own words.
**Payer decision moment:** step 2, the deposit. Payer is the user.
**Honest ending:** referral to another man in his circle, or a second booking for the next occasion. The first cohort measures whether either happens; it is not assumed.

## 8. APPROACH AND MILESTONE

**Recommended approach:** build the page as the experiment artifact through Stages 3 to 6 (economics, offer, brand and naming, page copy and build), fulfil the first five nights concierge-style with no capital purchase before the pass. The founder builds the page independently; the payment link and form are off-the-shelf.
**Estimate range:** page and checkout 1 to 3 days once copy exists; consultation script, waiver, cue cards 2 to 4 days; bakery partner agreement 1 to 2 weeks of outside waiting; experiment 30 days from launch. External cost: about $1,500 ad spend plus $900 to $1,400 variable cost per delivered night, paid from the deposit and balance.
**Estimate assumptions:** founder builds the page; a bakery or caterer partner is found in the launch city inside two weeks; the two founders can each give one weekday evening and one weekend slot to setup and collection; no custom software.
**First milestone:** a decision-quality result at day 30: pass, fail or inconclusive against the fixed thresholds, with warm/cold tags on every deposit.
**Definition of MVP complete:** the day-30 decision is recorded in this packet's revision ledger with the sample, the observed behaviour and the decision taken.
