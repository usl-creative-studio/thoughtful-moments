## 3. CONFIRMED PROBLEM STATEMENT

**Problem statement:**
> Busy, high-earning men in committed relationships struggle to make their partner feel genuinely loved and prioritised because their careers consume the time, energy and planning capacity that intentional acts of service require, and the default fallback (spending money on a gift or a night out) does not read as effort to the partner.

**Notes on this problem statement:**
- The man is not short of money. He is short of planning capacity and skill. Money alone does not buy the outcome he wants, which is why an expensive gift underperforms.
- The founder's own value-equation table scored the DIY route 2/4 against done-for-you 4/4. That framing treats effort as a cost. The reframe carried forward: the partner does not value the massage, the tea or the meal; she values that he did it. Effort is the product. The service should remove every ounce of effort except the gesture itself.
- Pull-driven purchase: usually occasion-triggered (anniversary, birthday, Valentine's, "I have been absent lately").

**Problem source:** Direct experience (founder has lived it), supported by public evidence (divorce and drift statistics cited in the business plan; live research this session).
**Problem source, payer side:** Same as above.
**Problem confidence level:** High. Lived by the founder, consistent with public evidence, and the root cause is specific enough to design against.

## 5. SOLUTION DIRECTION

**What the user currently has or is proposing:**
A premium at-home experience delivered as a curated kit plus concierge service. Three experiences: afternoon tea (fine china, loose-leaf tea, specialty pastries from a partner bakery, cue cards), at-home massage (table, oils, candles, towels, roses, technique and conversation cue cards), and a home-cooked meal (custom recipe, sourced ingredients, drink pairing, cooking and discussion cue cards). Personalised consultation before, delivery and setup, collection after.

**Solution-to-problem alignment:**
Yes. It directly removes the planning-capacity and skill gap (the root cause) while preserving the man's own effort in the delivery, which is what the partner reads as love.

**Recommended adjustments or reframes:**
1. Treat effort as the feature, not the cost. The service removes everything except the gesture: consultation, sourcing, delivery, setup, collection and cleanup are done for him; the tea, the massage or the meal is done by him. This is the justification for pricing above done-for-you comparables and it should be the spine of the offer and the page.
2. Stage 2 should consider launching with one experience rather than three, since inventory (massage tables, china) and cue-card writing scale per experience and the budget is $10k.
3. Reconcile the per-experience price band with the $500/month subscription in the second document. Recommendation carried to Stage 3: lead with the per-experience premium; a subscription is a later retention play.

## 7. UNIQUE VALUE PROPOSITION (UVP)

**Confirmed UVP:**
> For high-earning men in committed relationships, who want their partner to feel truly prioritised but have no time or skill to plan the gesture, Thoughtful Moments provides a complete at-home experience he delivers with his own hands, by handling every part of the planning, sourcing, setup and cleanup so the only thing left for him to do is the part she remembers.

**Payer proposition (only where the payer is not the user):**
> Payer is the user.

**What makes this genuinely unique:**
Done-for-you services remove the man from the moment; date-night boxes leave all the work and none of the luxury. Thoughtful Moments removes everything except his effort, which is the one thing his partner actually wants from him.

**Alternatives the user is competing against:**
1. Conspicuous spend: bottle service, a birthday night out, jewellery ($2,500+; money spent, effort not perceived).
2. Done-for-you premium: booking a spa, private chef or in-home couples massage ($500 to $1,500).
3. Date-night subscription boxes ($20 to $119).
4. Flowers, dinner out, or doing nothing.

**UVP confidence level:** Medium. The differentiator is specific and defensible against every comparable found; the target buyer's reaction to it has not been observed.
